## Intro — intimate, restrained

I remember loving you when the room was barely warm.
I remember making shelter out of every storm.
You knew the man beneath the pressure, before the lights came on.
So tell me why you found your way back only after I was gone?

## Verse 1 — love before the fracture

We had a crib beside the mattress, bills beside the bread,
small dreams in a borrowed room, big plans inside our heads.
I gave you every honest hour, every piece I had,
stood beside you in the hunger, never asked you to pretend it wasn’t bad.

I called you my forever when forever looked like stress,
when the rent came due on Friday and the cupboards answered less.
You said you loved the man in me, not the money in my hand,
said the hard days made us stronger, said together was the plan.

But pressure has a way of showing what the promises conceal,
and the silence in your messages became a wound I couldn’t heal.
You walked away from struggle like it made me someone small,
then gave the part of me I trusted to the one I called my brother in it all.

I won’t dress it up as fate, I won’t pretend I didn’t know.
Some betrayals wear a familiar face before they go.
I carried our child’s future while you carried out the lie.
You left me with the truth, then asked me not to ask you why.

## Pre-chorus — restrained anger rising

I was there when the lights were low.
I was there when the cold winds blew.
You didn’t leave because the love was gone.
You left when the hard days lasted too long.

## Chorus — melodic, direct, anthemic

You came back when I won, when the lights hit my name.
You came back when the struggle turned to fortune and acclaim.
But you didn’t love the hard times, you loved the headline.
You want the man the world can see, not the one you left behind.

You came back when I won — but the door don’t feel the same.
You can’t buy back the nights you traded for a different name.
I loved you in the valley; you returned to claim the view.
You came back when I won, but I don’t belong to you.

## Verse 2 — the betrayal and the rebuild

I saw the two of you move quiet, thought the quiet meant respect,
while I was counting every payment, trying to keep the future checked.
I gave him a place at the table, gave him access to my pain,
never thought the hand I trusted would be reaching through your name.

You knew the nights I couldn’t sleep, the weight behind my eyes,
knew I was building from the floor with no applause and no disguise.
But the first clean opportunity became your exit sign,
and the friend who knew my history stepped across the final line.

I did not turn the child into a weapon, did not make a public scene,
kept the promise that a father’s love should stay clean.
I learned to separate the damage from the life we had to raise,
learned a man can hold his boundaries and still show grace.

Then the rooms got bigger, then the checks began to clear,
then my name started traveling through the city and the sphere.
Suddenly your memories were warmer, suddenly you knew the door,
suddenly the woman who left me said she loved me even more.

But I remember every version of the truth you made me face.
Fame can put a shine on everything; it cannot change the place.
You don’t miss the man who fought to keep the family whole.
You miss the way the world now treats the man you let go.

But love is not a lifetime pass to walk back through my scars.
Regret is not a time machine, and money is not a heart.

You can be the mother of my child and still not be for me.

## Performance direction

The intro should sound intimate and wounded without begging. Verse 1 is conversational and detailed, with KTD remembering the shared hardship before the betrayal is named. The first pre-chorus should compress the emotion into short, repeatable lines. The chorus must be melodic, direct and memorable, with the title delivered as a clear diagnosis rather than a scream.

Verse 2 should become more rhythmically assertive when KTD describes the friend crossing the line and the later return after fame. The vocal should carry anger through articulation, not through constant shouting. The bridge must be the emotional center: KTD admits the love was real, protects the child from the conflict, and establishes the boundary that forgiveness does not restore access.

The final chorus should feel liberated rather than vengeful. The last line of the outro must sound like a decision already made: KTD is not denying the past, but he is no longer available to the person who abandoned him when the future was uncertain.

## Editorial guardrails

The song is a first-person artistic narrative. It does not identify the woman, the friend, the child, dates, locations or private details. It does not threaten, expose or encourage retaliation. The co-parenting reference remains respectful and does not place responsibility for adult betrayal on the child. The composition is original and must not imitate a real artist or reproduce any lyric, melody, sample or recognizable arrangement from the reference video.
