## Creative correction

The previous nu metal / rap-rock proof is rejected and archived. This version must sound unmistakably like KTD's established portfolio: old-school Black music rap, boom bap swing, narrative authority, warm soul harmony and a controlled but forceful medium-low rap performance. Do not use distorted rock guitars as the main identity, stadium-rock drums, EDM drops, pop-rock belting or nu metal architecture.

The narrative remains “I Won't Waste This Life”: KTD looks at loss, family pressure, closed doors and the years he nearly lost, then chooses presence, discipline and life. The emotional tone is hard-earned hope, not cheerful pop. The track should be commercial and memorable because the pocket, hook and soul harmony are strong—not because it imitates a real artist.

## Beat DNA

Build an original 1990s-informed boom bap beat with human swing and modern low-end translation. Use a dry kick with a rounded body, a crisp snare with a short room, economical swung hi-hats, ghost notes, subtle percussion and a thick bassline that follows the pocket. Add an original Rhodes, Wurlitzer, muted soul-guitar phrase or warm electric-piano chord loop with dusty harmonic texture. A restrained gospel/soul response may appear in the chorus, but it must remain original and secondary to KTD's rap.

The groove should nod to classic Black music rap through swing, chopped-feeling rhythmic punctuation and soulful harmony without using samples, recognizable interpolations, lifted drum breaks or any identifiable arrangement from a real artist. Create the musical feeling from newly generated instruments and original patterns.

Suggested harmonic loop for the verse: `F#m7 – Dmaj7 – Aadd9 – E6`. The pre-chorus can simplify to `Dmaj7 – E6 – C#7sus4 – C#7`. The chorus can open to `Aadd9 – E6 – F#m7 – Dmaj7`, with a warm bass movement and a sung-rap hook rather than a rock melody. The bridge can strip to `Bm7 – Dmaj7 – Aadd9 – E6` with Rhodes, bass and rim/snare before the final chorus.

## Vocal direction

KTD must sound like a rapper first. Use a medium-low, frontal, chest-led delivery with clear diction, internal rhyme, controlled aggression, deliberate pauses and short double-time bursts. The verses should be primarily rhythmic rap with only small melodic inflections at line endings. The pre-chorus may become more melodic, but it must still feel like KTD speaking through pitch. The chorus should be a soulful rap hook with a memorable repeated title, not a pop-rock vocal performance.

Use a dry, close lead in the verses. Add subtle doubles only on key phrases. In the chorus, layer a lead, a low double and a small original soul/gospel response on “I won’t waste this life.” Keep the lead dominant and intelligible. Do not imitate any real rapper or singer, and do not use biometric voice cloning.

## Mix direction

Keep kick, snare, bass and lead vocal centered. Let the kick have dry punch around the low and low-mid body, let the snare crack without harshness, and use bass saturation to create audible harmonics without muddying the Rhodes. Preserve swing and transient life. The drum bus may receive gentle glue compression and parallel presence, but do not flatten the groove.

The Rhodes and soul layers should occupy the sides moderately while leaving the center available for KTD. Use short room or plate reverb, filtered and controlled; avoid large rock halls. Vocal delay should be short and musical, appearing at selected phrase endings rather than washing the whole verse. The chorus can widen through doubles, choir and keys, not through extreme stereo widening of the low end.

Target a punchy but breathable first proof, approximately −11 to −9 LUFS integrated depending on crest factor, with true peak below −1 dBTP after final mastering decisions.
